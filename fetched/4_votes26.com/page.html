How is the forecast made?

Every polling station is compared with how the same station voted last time. From the stations already counted, the model learns where each previous list's voters went and how many turned out, and applies that to every station not yet counted, sized by the voters expected there today.

The result goes through the threshold, the surplus agreements and the Bader–Ofer method. The range shows how far the picture can still move: wide early in the count, narrowing as more stations come in.

The technical detail
The data set
Every 2026 station and its electorate; the same station's 2022 result (matched by station, then polling place, then locality); 12 historical signals from 2015–2022 (independent Otzma Yehudit, New Right, Zehut, Yamina, right and centre-left strongholds); election-day turnout reports per station; the double envelopes as their own unit; and election-night snapshots from 2019–2022 for calibration.
The model
List p's share at station s is ⁦Σₖ x[s,k]·B[k,p] + u[L,p]⁩: x are the previous lists' shares at the station, B the transition matrix (the share of each previous list's voters now voting for each list), u a locality deviation. B is fitted by vote-weighted ridge regression on the counted stations and shrunk toward the running tally when little is counted.
The weights
Ridge strength 10 votes per previous-list group; 100 for the starting assumption on where new lists' votes come from, and 400 votes for each such list's previous level; 3,000 for the historical signals; locality deviation shrunk by 600 votes; locality turnout correction shrunk by 400 votes; previous lists under 1% pooled as 'other'.
Turnout
The votes expected at each station not yet counted come from today's turnout report for that station, corrected by the turnout change measured in the counted stations.
Uncertainty
320 simulations (20 refits × 16 draws): a locality bootstrap; locality shocks borrowed from the 15 most similar counted localities (scale 0.6); and a shock common to everything not yet counted (12%), growing when the counted stations are unrepresentative (×25 the composition distance) and when a list's electorate is not yet counted (×4). The double-envelope volume has a log-SD of 0.18. Each simulation goes through the 3.25% threshold, surplus agreements and Bader–Ofer; the range is 80% of the simulations.
The path to 100%
On the trend charts, the running count closes on the forecast along a curve fitted to 62 snapshots from past nights: ⁦D(x) = 0.085·(1−x)^0.87⁩, with x the share of stations counted. The forecast's range narrows with the square root of what is left to count.

Israel election results 2026, live

Votes26 tracks the results of the election for Israel's 26th Knesset, held on Tuesday 27 October 2026. From the morning: turnout by hour, sector and town. At 22:00: the TV channels' exit polls. Once the Central Elections Committee publishes its first files, the page updates on its own: the vote count, results by polling station and town, a seat forecast for every list, the blocs and who clears the threshold — through to the double envelopes.

Unlike the raw count, the forecast compares every counted ballot box with its own result in the previous election, correcting the bias of the first boxes counted. The forecast is not an official result.

Live vote-count updates: every file the Central Elections Committee publishes is picked up here within minutes — the national results, the share of stations counted and the seats. On the turnout page: Israel's election map for the 26th Knesset, with turnout in every town.

When will the 2026 election results be published?
Israel votes for the 26th Knesset on Tuesday, 27 October 2026. Polls close at 22:00 and the Central Elections Committee's first results usually appear around midnight. Votes26 updates on its own with every new file.
When are the exit polls published?
The TV channels' exit polls are published when polls close at 22:00. Votes26 shows them separately; its seat forecast rests only on the count at the polling stations.
Where can I follow the election results live?
On Votes26.com: the count for every list, a seat forecast, the blocs and who clears the threshold, updating all night. Official results are published by the Central Elections Committee.
What is the difference between the vote count and the forecast?
The Central Elections Committee count shows only the stations counted so far, and they are not counted in random order. The forecast compares every counted station with its own previous result and estimates the final outcome, with a range.
How many seats does a government need?
The Knesset has 120 members, so 61 seats or more is a majority. The threshold is 3.25% of valid votes.
What is the electoral threshold?
A list needs at least 3.25% of valid votes to enter the Knesset. A list below it wins no seats, and the seats are shared among the lists above it.
How are the seats allocated?
The 120 seats go to the lists above the threshold by the Bader–Ofer method, with the surplus agreements pairs of lists signed before the election.
What are the double envelopes, and when are the final results published?
Votes cast by soldiers, diplomats, hospital patients, prisoners and voters with disabilities are counted after the regular stations, and can move a seat. The official final results are published within eight days of election day.
How do I see the results in my town?
On "My station", search for a town or a station to get its results in 2019–2022, list by list, against the national result. On election day the turnout map shows every town against 2022.
